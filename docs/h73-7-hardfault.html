Hypothesis & mechanism

Catalogue pixels that are hardest to see (weak 1 m-LiDAR step, deep basement, gentle detrended slope) are the catalogue subpopulation most similar to expert-added hidden faults; up-weighting them in a physical-feature ranker shifts dotted emission toward covered piedmont faults instead of easy bedrock/range-front traces.

Fold-fair HistGradientBoosting PU ranker (300 iters, lr 0.06, 63 leaves) on 41 physical channels (19 official GeoDAWN/topo bands, 10 LiDAR scarp bands, 8 radiometric/extension bands, 4 unsupervised H73-1 components); positives = visible catalogue (48,340 px) weighted 1+2h; negatives = 250,000 footprint px >5 px from VISIBLE faults only; no distance-to-catalogue feature, no scored-raster input. Frozen dotted emission: 37,654 dots, 2.5 px spacing, >2.0 px from visible (production: full) catalogue. Production surface retrained on the full 60,988-px catalogue with the identical recipe.

Named non-fault mimic: Lithologic contacts and alluvial-fan surfaces (magnetic/gravity/radiometric signature of covered faults without faulting), plus catalogue mapping bias itself - where mappers looked is not where hidden faults are.
