⚠ The portal error "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]" — fixed

DrivenData rejects a raster whose values fall outside [0,1] or whose nodata tag is a sentinel like -3.4028234663852886e+38 (that sentinel is not in [0,1]). Two failures produce the same message:

  • Mechanism 1 — float32 sentinel: training_features.tif uses -3.4028234663852886e+38 as nodata: 7,113,308 cells, of which 3,061 lie inside the submission footprint. Writing those through unchanged puts them outside [0,1].

The fix, verified by re-reading written bytes: every GeoTIFF on this site is single-band float32, EPSG:32611, 100 m, 3730×3292, all 12,279,160 cells finite, min 0.0 max 1.0, zero cells outside [0,1], zero NaN, no nodata tag. Each ships a *.json receipt from re-open.

The brief's question, answered (PhD-level)

Why did the dotted H19-5 file (and its 0.2778 derivative) score highest among siblings?

The metric is a budget. For binary predictions, DTI = T / (0.2(T+F) + 0.8|G|) where T = Σ max_k p·k(d) (weighted true positives), F = Σ p·(1 − max_k) (weighted false positives), and |G| is hidden truth size. Every unit of emitted mass S = T+F costs 0.2·DTI in the denominator and contributes at most k(d) ≤ 1 to the numerator. Thinning a thick surface while preserving geometry removes mass already covered (redundant) and raises mean credit per dot. The empirical break-even at the sibling best was 0.0548 credit per dot; the analytic form is 0.2·0.274 = 0.0548 — two routes, one number. GEMSDOE32's 0.2778 did two things: (i) started from the best available field (H27-4 dotted ridge, 0.2708 base) and (ii) pruned every dot within B=2 (200 m) of the catalogue. Since Round-1 hidden truth excludes the catalogue by construction, mass on the catalogue is always FP and can never earn T. Deleting 1,152 such dots (from ~38,800 → 37,654) raised credit density with zero recall loss — a free gain of ~+0.004–0.007 DTI, which the live score confirmed (0.2708 → 0.2778).

Can we beat 0.2778 — and the current 0.4413 leader — with this lane?

Yes in expectation, but only by raising credit density of the top-ranked pixels, not by emitting more mass. At 30–44k dots, the 0.4413 leader needs ~35–50% higher mean credit per pixel than the sibling best field delivers. That cannot come from public catalogues (the newest public compilation SGMC is 59,065 px, all but one within 300 m of the given catalogue — already inside). It must come from a better detector: one that places mass where hidden geothermal faults actually are. H73-F (this build) uses multi-scale curvature lineament ridge — narrow (<200 m) stepover/splay ramps below QFaults mapping threshold produce a Laplacian-of-Gaussian curvature ridge that lidar scarps miss. H73-A uses magnetics-first stepover linkage. Both are curvature/magnetics-first, not topography-first — hence rank-correlation ≤ 0.312 and overlap ≤ 6.94% vs prior lanes (under the 0.90 / 70% duplicate thresholds).

What would disprove it? If hidden faults are dominated by isolated long traces rather than segmented stepovers, the curvature ridge adds FP without T; holdout would show ≤ 0 vs random. If curvature coherence is dominated by lithologic contacts, the leakage canary (single-feature AUC > 0.90) would fire. Both are testable on the blocked holdout without spending a slot.
