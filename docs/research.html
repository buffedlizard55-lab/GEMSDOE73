1. Why 0.2778 was the sibling group’s ceiling — and why it was not a “model” win

Across 78 sibling repos (GEMSDOE through 78GEMSDOE, plus numbered forks) the highest owner-reported DTI before 2026-10-04 was 0.2778 for h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros (GEMSDOE32) and 0.2710 for h36-1-rung30-blind-r1 (GEMSDOE28). Both descend from the same base lineage: gems25-dotted-h19-5-d2-8 (0.2600) → gems24-h25-1-dotted-h19-5-d1-5 (0.2477) → h27-4-r1-solo-d2-8 (0.2708) → pruned variants.

What the 0.2778 actually did: it deleted mass. Starting from the 0.2708 base (37,654–44,090 dots depending on version), it applied flank prune B=2 (remove every dot within 2 px = 200 m of the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue). Because Round-1 hidden truth is defined to exclude the catalogue, any dot on the catalogue has k(d)=0 for all hidden truth and contributes strictly to FP (cost 0.2 per unit mass) — it can never be a TP. Deleting 1,152 such dots raised mean credit per dot from ~0.053 to ~0.058, moving the file past the break-even bar k > 0.2·DTI ≈ 0.055 at that DTI. The arithmetic gain is ΔDTI ≈ (ΔT − 0.2·ΔS)/D; with ΔS = −1,152 and ΔT ≈ 0 (pruned dots were never near hidden truth), ΔDTI ≈ +0.004–0.007, which the live increment 0.2708→0.2778 confirms.

Why nothing higher within the group? Every sibling that tried to add mass (topo-gap-closure-t-v2, submodular multipysics 46,090 px, dilcond-oof-v1 0.1223, topo-gap v2 0.2449) kept the same detector field (dotted H19-5 or lidar-scarp ridge) and added dots where that field was strong — i.e. where the catalogue already runs. Their marginal k was typically 0.03–0.05, below the bar 0.0548 at 0.27, so DTI fell or stalled. The one exception that added off-catalogue dots with higher k was H36-1 rung30 blind R1 (0.2710) — it placed rung dots at stepovers between parallel segments, a hint that off-catalogue linkage, not surface following, is the lever.

PhD synthesis: the group hit a detector ceiling, not an emission ceiling. Their emission rule was optimal for their field (greedy max-coverage of the truth-scatter-smoothed surface won +0.0140 on paired draws vs incumbent at matched mass, 16/16 draws positive; greedy surface-only lost −0.0043, 0/12 — GEMSDOE32 emission_models_mc.json). But the field itself (dotted ridge + lidar scarp) lacks the hidden geothermal signal. The public leaderboard jump to 0.4413 (xiaofanhu, 26 submissions) and 0.3904 (cfld__, 14 submissions) with few attempts implies a materially better detector — likely a trained U-Net with multi-channel geophysics (TMI vertical gradient, RTP, isostatic gravity, strain) rather than heuristic thresholds. The gap to 0.4413 at same mass is ≈ +35–50% mean credit per pixel, requiring a new physical cue the sibling hand-crafted fields do not contain.

2. The metric, exactly

Distance-weighted Tversky with α=0.2 β=0.8 R=3 px (300 m) and triangular kernel k(d)=max(1−d/R,0):

TP_w = Σ_g max_{x:d≤R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))
FP_w = Σ_x p(x)·[1 − max_g k(d(x,g))]
FN_w = |G| − TP_w
DTI  = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)

Equivalently DTI = T / (0.2(T+S−M) + 0.8|G|) where S=Σ p and M=Σ p·k_near. Worked example T=3.00 F=1.89 K=5.00 → DTI=0.60 reproduced in tests/test_metric.py. The break-even marginal credit is k > α·DTI (corrected 2026-10-04 from the earlier αs/(1−αs) form — see GEMSDOE32 IR-32-BAR-01).

Scoring detail: known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are masked (organizer thread 11516). Only hidden faults count toward |G|; mass on the catalogue is pure FP.

3. What geothermal vents need geologically — and why faults are a proxy

NW Nevada geothermal systems (e.g. Dixie Valley, Brady Hot Springs) are hosted in transtensional stepovers between Walker Lane / Central Nevada Seismic Belt faults: a releasing bend or relay ramp between en echelon segments creates dilatation, fracture permeability, and a chimney of upwelling fluid. The INGENIOUS project (Great Basin Center for Geothermal Energy) frames this as structurally-controlled permeability — not every fault, but faults with (i) late Quaternary slip, (ii) dilation (normal + strike-slip), (iii) intersection/termination. Source: https://gbcge.org/current-projects/ingenious/ and GeoDAWN study (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ).

Why catalogue faults miss this: USGS QFaults and NBMG INGENIOUS map surface scarps. Where basin fill buries the scarp (Walker Lake paleo-shoreline, Lahontan sands), or where slip is distributed across a stepover, there is no scarp but there is permeability at depth. Microseismicity (ComCat), magnetics (basement offset), and gravity (density contrast) see it; lidar alone does not. Hence a lidar-first detector systematically underperforms on blind geothermal faults.

4. How we differ from every sibling lane

Sibling lanes clustered into three families: (1) dotted-ridge / power-law-budget multiline (H19-4/19-5), (2) lidar-scarp ridge (7GEMSDOE, 47GEMSDOE, 48GEMSDOE), (3) strain/thermal-pop openness (20GEMSDOE). All follow topography or scalar strain magnitude and emit solid or dotted surfaces. H73-A follows magnetic second-derivative edge + gravity-slope concordance and emits only linkage at segment tips (relay ramps, wing cracks). Quantitatively: rank-correlation <0.30 and overlap <35% within 3 px vs any registry raster — checked on the surface before placement and on final dots per PARALLEL-RUN PROTOCOL §1.

5. Leakage canary

Every single-feature AUC is tested on the spatially-blocked holdout before trusting any result. AUC >0.90 triggers a leakage flag until proven otherwise (e.g. distance-to-known-fault features leak trivially). Our primary uses synthetic features, so no single-channel AUC can leak catalogue geometry; the canary passes by construction. When the full 19-channel GeoDAWN stack is placed (see sources), we will publish per-channel holdout AUCs in evidence/leakage.json.
